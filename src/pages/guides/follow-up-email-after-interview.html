How to Write a Follow-Up Email After an Interview

A short, specific follow-up email is a simple way to thank the interviewer, show you were listening, and keep in touch while they decide. Here is what to write and when to send it.

Published October 20, 2026 · 6 min read

There are three different follow-ups

  1. The thank-you email, sent soon after the interview, often the same day or the next working day.
  2. The check-in, sent if the date they gave you for a decision has passed with no news.
  3. The final follow-up, sent once more if you still have not heard back after the check-in.

Each one has a different goal, so keep them separate rather than combining everything into one long message.

1. The thank-you email

Keep it to a few sentences. Include:

  • A thank-you for their time, with the job title.
  • One specific detail from the conversation, which shows you were paying attention and makes your email different from a template.
  • A short line confirming your interest, and an offer to provide anything else they need.
Example

Subject: Thank you – Marketing Coordinator interview

Dear Ms. Patel,

Thank you for taking the time to speak with me about the Marketing Coordinator position today. I especially enjoyed hearing about the plans for the spring campaign and how the team splits work between content and events.

The conversation confirmed my interest in the role. If it would help, I am happy to share more writing samples.

Kind regards,
Alex Morgan

If several people interviewed you, a short individual note to each person you have contact details for is a nice touch. Change the specific detail in each one.

2. The check-in

If they told you when to expect a decision, wait until that date has passed. If they did not give a date, many people wait about a week. Keep the check-in polite and short, and do not suggest they have forgotten you.

Example

Subject: Following up: Marketing Coordinator interview

Dear Ms. Patel,

I hope you are well. I am following up on my interview on [date] for the Marketing Coordinator position. I am still very interested in the role and wanted to ask whether there is any update on the timeline.

Thank you again for your time.

Kind regards,
Alex Morgan

3. The final follow-up

If there is still no reply after another week or so, one last message is reasonable. Keep it gracious. It leaves a good impression even if this role does not work out.

Example

Subject: Marketing Coordinator role – final follow-up

Dear Ms. Patel,

I wanted to follow up one last time about the Marketing Coordinator position. I understand hiring decisions can take time. If the role has been filled, I would appreciate knowing, and I would be glad to be considered for future openings.

Best regards,
Alex Morgan

Mistakes to avoid

  • Sending a generic note. One specific detail from the interview makes a big difference.
  • Re-arguing the interview. A follow-up is not the place to give a long new answer to a question you struggled with. One sentence is fine if it is important.
  • Following up too often. Two follow-ups after the thank-you email is usually the limit.
  • Getting the name or job title wrong. Check spelling before you press send.
  • Sounding demanding. “I need an answer by Friday” rarely helps unless you have another offer with a real deadline. If you do, say so politely and honestly.
