How to use the resume bullet generator

  1. Write one thing you did in your own words. It can be rough, like “helped customers with returns”.
  2. Add the tools or skills you used, if any, and choose past tense for an old job or present tense for your current one.
  3. Pick a tone and select Generate bullets. Use Show other wording to try different action verbs.
  4. Edit the version you like. Replace anything in [brackets] with a real number or result, or delete it.

What the three tones do

  • Professional keeps your full description and adds your skills or industry for context.
  • Concise trims the bullet to the action and the object. It suits a one-page resume.
  • Achievement-focused adds a placeholder for a measurable result, so you remember to show impact. The tool never fills in a number for you.

Example

You enter

I was responsible for answering customer emails and phone calls. Skills: Zendesk.

Possible bullets

Answered customer emails and phone calls, using Zendesk.
Responded to customer emails and phone calls in a retail setting.
Used Zendesk to handle customer emails and phone calls.

Then make it specific: “Answered 40–60 customer emails a day in Zendesk” is stronger, but only if the number is true.

Limitations

  • The generator follows grammar rules and a built-in list of common verbs. Unusual phrasing can come out awkward, so always read the result.
  • It works best with one task at a time. If you paste several sentences, only the first one is used.
  • It cannot judge whether a bullet suits a particular job posting. Tailoring is up to you.

Frequently asked questions

Does this tool use AI?

No. It uses transparent rules: it finds the action in your sentence, removes filler phrases such as “I was responsible for”, and rebuilds the sentence with a strong verb. That is also why it never invents details.

Is my information stored or sent anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser. What you type is not sent to a server and is gone when you close or reload the page.

Should resume bullets be in past or present tense?

A common convention is present tense for your current job and past tense for previous jobs. Choose the tense setting to match.

How many bullet points should each job have?

There is no fixed rule. Many resumes use three to five bullets for recent roles and fewer for older ones. Focus on the tasks most relevant to the job you are applying for.

Why are there placeholders in brackets?

Numbers and outcomes make bullets stronger, but only you know the real figures. The brackets remind you to add a verified result or delete the phrase.
